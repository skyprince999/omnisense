‘Near’ isn't a number. Metres are.

A detector gives you a box, not a distance. The distance comes out of geometry: where an object's tyres meet the road, plus the horizon line, the camera height and the focal length. That yields metres — measured from the front bumper, not from the lens and not from the bottom of the picture. Known vehicle widths cross-check the answer.

Track the same object across frames and the distance turns into closing speed and time-to-collision. That is what a nudge is made of.

What the lens can see, from the bumper outward

Seen from above, the camera's reach is a cone with a hole punched in the near end. The raw lens is a 140°-diagonal fisheye; the geometry only holds after undistortion, over a narrower rectilinear frame — and it holds best near the optical centre, which is exactly where the lead vehicle sits.

3One frame

From a pixel row to metres

Two independent routes, run on every box of every frame. Route A reads the row where the tyres touch the road against the horizon. Route B reads the box width against a known vehicle width. They fail in different places, so they are fused by confidence rather than picked between.

The frame the detector sees — normalised coordinates
  • v₀ — the assumed horizon, and the only vertical reference the geometry has. Move the pitch slider and a red line appears: that is where the horizon really is.
  • v_n — the contact row, where the tyres meet the road. This single number is Route A's entire input.
  • w_n — the box width. This single number is Route B's entire input.

Two channels, on purpose. The metric TTC needs the calibration to be right. The scale-invariant one, TTC ≈ 2 / (d/dt·ln area), comes purely from how fast the box is growing — no focal length, no camera height, no ego speed. While the calibration slot still says calibrated: false, that second channel is the one to trust, and the two agreeing is what makes a nudge safe to fire.

Honest status. The horizon is still assumed at v₀ = 0.50 and the focal length is derived from the sensor spec, not measured — so absolute metres carry real error (stage 3's pitch slider shows how much). What is already trustworthy is the relative behaviour: which object is closer, whether a gap is shrinking, and the scale-invariant TTC. Running the benchmark in dashcam_distance_benchmark.md and setting calibrated: true fixes the absolutes with no code change.
